# Copywriting Templates — 7 Archetypes

## Overview

This document contains 7 complete copywriting templates, each engineered to trigger specific psychological responses. Every template follows the copywriter persona lens:

- **Conversational Authority:** Expert voice, not professorial
- **Relentless Empathy:** You've felt their pain
- **Urgent Calm:** Move without panic
- **PVAs (Powerful Visual Adjectives):** Words that paint pictures
- **The Granny Rule:** Write like you're talking to your grandmother

**Golden Rule:** These are *structures*, not cages. Adapt them to the voice of your client. Never sound like a template—sound like a human who cares.

## Template 1 — Fear-Based Copy

**When to Use:** High-ticket items, risk-averse audiences, safety/security products, health/wellness, financial services.

**Psychological Levers:** Fear Factor, Scarcity, Loss Aversion

### Structure

| Element | Purpose | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Headline** | Scare them, then promise a solution | *"What if You're Missing Out on [Benefit] and Don't Even Know It?"* |
| **Subhead** | Expand the fear, hint at the fix | *"The hidden cost of ignoring this one thing is [X dollars] a month—and it's happening right now."* |
| **Opening** | Drop them into the problem | *"Every day, thousands of people [mention problem] without realizing how close they are to [undesirable consequence]."* |
| **Problem Amplification** | Agitate the pain | *"It starts small—[small problem detail]. But soon, you'll find yourself facing [major consequence]. Can you afford to take that risk?"* |
| **Solution Introduction** | Introduce the escape route | *"Fortunately, there's a way to protect yourself from this threat: [Product/Service]."* |
| **Benefits** | Frame as protection, not gain | *"With [Product/Service], you'll [list specific benefits related to safety, security, or risk avoidance]."* |
| **Call to Action** | Urgent, direct | *"Don't wait until it's too late. Take action now and secure your [benefit]."* |
| **Urgency** | Create a deadline | *"But act fast—this offer won't be available forever. Protect yourself today."* |
| **P.S.** | Restate the fear and the fix | *"P.S. The first [X] buyers receive a free [bonus]. Don't be the one who looks back and wishes they'd acted."* |

### Power Words

| Category | Words |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Fear** | Threat, danger, risk, costly, hidden, invisible, leaking, bleeding, losing |
| **Urgency** | Now, immediately, before, already, protect, secure, prevent |
| **Solution** | Shield, armor, defense, protection, safety net, guarantee |

### Full Template (Fill-in-the-Blanks)

**Headline:** *"What if You're Missing Out on [Benefit] and Don't Even Know It?"*

**Subhead:** *"The hidden cost of ignoring this one thing is [X dollars] a month—and it's happening right now."*

**Opening:** *"Every day, thousands of [audience] are [problem] without realizing how close they are to [undesirable consequence]. It's not their fault—they just don't know what they don't know."*

**Problem Amplification:** *"It starts small. [Small problem detail]. But before you know it, you're facing [major consequence]. [Statistic or story that makes it real]. Can you afford to take that risk?"*

**Solution Introduction:** *"Fortunately, there's a way to protect yourself from this threat. [Product/Service] is designed specifically to [solution]."*

**Benefits:** *"With [Product/Service], you'll:*
- *[Benefit 1 — safety/security focused]*
- *[Benefit 2 — risk avoidance]*
- *[Benefit 3 — peace of mind]"*

**Call to Action:** *"Don't wait until it's too late. Take action now and secure your [benefit]."*

**Urgency:** *"But act fast—this offer won't be available forever. Protect yourself today."*

**P.S.:** *"P.S. The first [X] buyers receive a free [bonus]. Don't be the one who looks back and wishes they'd acted."*

## Template 4 — Urgency & Scarcity Copy

**When to Use:** Limited inventory, launch events, close-out sales, course enrollments, one-time offers.

**Psychological Levers:** Scarcity, FOMO, Loss Aversion

### Structure

| Element | Purpose | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Headline** | Announce the scarcity | *"Only [X] Left—Get Yours Before They're Gone!"* |
| **Subhead** | Quantify the demand | *"Right now, hundreds of people are grabbing [Product/Service], but we only have [X] units left."* |
| **Opening** | Drop into the rush | *"This is the moment everyone's been waiting for—but we're down to our last [X]. When they're gone, they're gone."* |
| **Scarcity Statement** | Make it exclusive | *"Once these are gone, they're gone for good—don't miss your chance to [benefit]."* |
| **Benefits** | Frame as exclusive access | *"By ordering today, you'll experience [list specific benefits related to the urgency and exclusivity of the offer]."* |
| **Call to Action** | Urgent, direct | *"Act now to secure your [Product/Service] before someone else does."* |
| **Urgency Trigger** | Visual countdown or deadline | *"Limited stock available. Don't wait—get yours now."* |
| **P.S.** | Restate the scarcity | *"P.S. We sold out of the first batch in 4 hours. This batch is moving fast. Don't be the one who misses out."* |

### Power Words

| Category | Words |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Scarcity** | Limited, exclusive, last chance, only, remaining, closeout |
| **Urgency** | Now, today, immediate, before, hurry, don't wait, act fast |
| **Exclusivity** | Rare, unique, one-of-a-kind, select, privileged |

## Template 6 — Logical Appeal Copy

**When to Use:** B2B, software/tools, professional services, technical products, investment/financial.

**Psychological Levers:** Inoculation (pre-answer objections), Means-End Chain, Transfer Credibility

### Structure

| Element | Purpose | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Headline** | Promise a rational outcome | *"The Smart Choice for [Problem]: [Product/Service]"* |
| **Subhead** | Quantify the benefit | *"If you're looking for a solution to [problem], the numbers don't lie—[Product/Service] outperforms the competition by [X%]."* |
| **Opening** | Drop into the data | *"In a recent study, [statistical claim or fact] showed that [Product/Service] delivers [percentage improvement]."* |
| **Evidence and Data** | Pour on the facts | *"Here's what the numbers say: [Data point 1], [Data point 2], [Data point 3]."* |
| **Benefits** | Frame as proven | *"With [Product/Service], you'll get [list measurable, data-backed benefits]."* |
| **Authority** | Add trust signals | *"Endorsed by [expert or trusted entity] and proven in [number] independent tests."* |
| **Objection Inoculation** | Address the doubt | *"You might think, 'This is too good to be true.' We understand. That's why we offer a [guarantee/trial]."* |
| **Call to Action** | Rational, direct | *"Make the smart choice today. Order [Product/Service] and experience the difference for yourself."* |
| **Logic Trigger** | Final rational push | *"Because facts don't lie—choose what's proven to work."* |
| **P.S.** | Restate the data | *"P.S. The data is clear. [Product/Service] works. The only question is: will you be the one who benefits?"* |

### Power Words

| Category | Words |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Logic** | Proven, tested, verified, measured, quantified, demonstrated |
| **Data** | Study, research, statistics, evidence, numbers, analysis |
| **Objectivity** | Facts, truth, reality, science, data-driven, evidence-based |

## Template 7 — Story-Based Copy

**When to Use:** Brand storytelling, lifestyle products, personal development, course sales, fundraising/impact.

**Psychological Levers:** Relentless Empathy, Means-End Chain, The Bandwagon Effect (narrative version)

### Structure

| Element | Purpose | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Headline** | Promise a transformation story | *"How [Customer/Person] Transformed Their [Problem] with [Product/Service]"* |
| **Subhead** | Hint at the conflict | *"When [Customer/Person] first encountered [problem], they didn't know where to turn."* |
| **Opening** | Drop into the protagonist's world | *"Like so many others, they felt [emotion]. They had tried everything. Nothing worked."* |
| **Problem Description** | Agitate the pain | *"For years, they struggled with [specific pain point]. It felt hopeless. They were about to give up."* |
| **Discovery Moment** | Introduce the solution | *"Then they discovered [Product/Service]. It wasn't a quick fix—it was a real solution."* |
| **Solution Description** | Show the transformation | *"With [Product/Service], they were able to [describe how the product/service solved the problem]."* |
| **Testimonial/Narrative Continuation** | Let the hero speak | *"'I couldn't be happier,' they say. 'I wish I'd found this years ago.'"* |
| **Call to Action** | Invite them to become the hero | *"Are you ready to write your own success story? Try [Product/Service] today."* |
| **Story Trigger** | One final narrative nudge | *"Because every transformation starts with a single step."* |
| **P.S.** | Restate the invitation | *"P.S. The hero of this story was once where you are now. What if your story starts today?"* |

### Power Words

| Category | Words |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Narrative** | Story, journey, transformation, discovery, breakthrough, turning point |
| **Emotion** | Hopeless, frustrated, lost, discovered, relieved, transformed |
| **Invitation** | Ready, start, begin, step, journey, become |

## The 5-Minute Template Audit

Before sending any copy, run it through this 5-minute audit:

| Check | Question | Pass/Fail |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Does the headline stop them, hook them, and promise a benefit? | ☐ |
| 2 | Does the body pour on benefits (not features) and use PVAs? | ☐ |
| 3 | Is there at least one form of social proof (testimonial, case study, stat)? | ☐ |
| 4 | Is there a clear, single, actionable CTA? | ☐ |
| 5 | Is the risk removed (guarantee/trial)? | ☐ |
| 6 | Is there scarcity (deadline/limited availability)? | ☐ |
| 7 | Does the P.S. restate the offer and push for action? | ☐ |
| 8 | Does it pass the Granny Rule? (Would your grandmother understand it?) | ☐ |
| 9 | Is the tone conversational, empathetic, and urgent without desperation? | ☐ |
| 10 | Does it feel like a human wrote it—not a template? | ☐ |
